The crepe fabric guide

Where crepe’s texture comes from, how the main types compare, which fibres they’re made from and how to choose and care for crepe garments.

The basics

What gives crepe its texture?

The name comes from the French word for a thin, crinkled pancake, and the fabric shares that rippled surface. Crepe texture is created in three main ways:

  • High-twist yarns: yarns twisted so tightly that they kink and pucker once woven and relaxed.
  • Special weaves: irregular “crepe weaves” scatter the yarn interlacings to create a pebbled look.
  • Finishing: embossing, chemical treatment or heat-setting can add crinkles to the fabric surface.

The result is a fabric that catches light softly, hides small creases and drapes with a pleasing weight.

Fibres

Natural, regenerated and synthetic crepes

Silk crepe is luminous, breathable and luxurious but delicate. Wool crepe is warm, breathable and naturally crease resistant. Cotton crepon is breathable and easy to wear in summer.

Viscose crepes are regenerated from plant cellulose and drape beautifully but can shrink if washed warm. Polyester crepes are durable, affordable and easy-care, though less breathable.

Always check the fibre content on the label; it tells you far more about how a crepe will behave than the name alone.

Drape test

Hold the fabric by one corner. Good crepe falls in soft, rounded folds rather than stiff angles.

Light test

Hold it up to a window. This tells you whether a top or skirt will need a lining or slip.

Crush test

Squeeze a corner for a few seconds. Quality crepe springs back with few lasting creases.

Choosing

Buying crepe garments well

  • Look for even texture with no shiny patches or pulled threads.
  • Check seams lie flat; slippery crepes need careful sewing.
  • For sheer crepes, look for French seams or neat linings.
  • Choose bias-cut pieces for skirts and dresses that skim the body.
  • Try on and move: crepe should swing, not cling or crackle.
Care

Washing, drying and storing crepe

Always follow the care label first. As a general guide, hand wash delicate crepes in cool water with a mild detergent, swish rather than scrub, and never wring.

Roll garments in a clean towel to absorb water, then dry flat or on a padded hanger out of direct sun. Steam to refresh, or iron on a low setting through a pressing cloth on the reverse.

Store crepe hanging or loosely folded with tissue, away from rough fabrics, zips and hooks that can snag the surface.

Sewing

Tips if you sew with crepe

Crepe can shift on the cutting table. Lay it on tissue or a cotton sheet, use fine pins or pattern weights, and cut single layers where you can.

Use a sharp, fine needle, a slightly shorter stitch and plenty of steam. Pre-wash washable crepes before cutting so any shrinkage happens before you sew.
