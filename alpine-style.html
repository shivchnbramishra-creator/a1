Alpine style: mountain calm, everyday elegance

How to dress with the ease and practicality of the mountains, in the city or on holiday, with layering ideas, a seasonal capsule and packing tips.

The idea

What makes a look feel alpine?

Alpine style is less a costume and more a mood: practical, calm and quietly polished. It borrows from the clothes people actually wear in the mountains, then refines them with beautiful fabrics.

  • Natural palette: snow, stone, pine, wood and wildflower tones.
  • Honest materials: wool, cotton, silk and textured crepes.
  • Smart layers: pieces that come on and off as the weather changes.
  • Sturdy footwear: boots and loafers that handle cobbles and paths.
Layering

Layering without bulk

The secret is to layer from thin to thick. Start with a fine base, such as a crepe jersey top or slim turtleneck, then add a knit or shirt, and finish with a coat or jacket.

Keep the thinnest, most fluid fabrics closest to the body and the most structured on the outside. A crepe midi skirt under a chunky knit and a long coat, finished with leather boots, is a classic alpine silhouette.

Accessories

Small details, big difference

Accessories bring warmth and personality to a simple wardrobe. A wool beret, a soft scarf in a single bright tone and a leather belt can change the feel of the same outfit from day to evening.

Choose a few pieces you love rather than many you rarely wear, and repeat them confidently.

Roll, don’t fold

Roll crepe and knits in your suitcase to limit creasing and save space.

Pack a steamer

A small travel steamer refreshes crepe and silk in minutes after a journey.

Three-colour rule

Pack around three main colours so every top works with every bottom.

Outerwear

Choosing a coat for mountain towns

A well-cut wool coat is the backbone of a cold-weather alpine wardrobe. Look for a length that covers your skirts and jackets, a collar you can turn up against the wind and a fabric with some weight.

For changeable spring and autumn days, a trench or a light quilted jacket layered over knitwear is more versatile.

Summer

Mountain summers

Summer in the mountains can be hot in the sun and cool in the shade. A breathable crepon or cotton dress with a light knit or overshirt over your arm covers both.

Pair dresses with comfortable sandals or trainers for village walks, and always carry a hat and a light layer for cooler evenings.
